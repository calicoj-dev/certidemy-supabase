## About this document

This is the **certification scheme** for the ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Internal Auditor -
AI credential: the published contract describing what the credential certifies,
how a candidate earns it, and how Certidemy keeps that decision fair, consistent
and defensible. It is written to align with the structure of the **ISO/IEC
17024:2026** framework for bodies operating certification of persons.

**A note on accreditation status.** Certidemy is **not currently an accredited
certification body**. This scheme is **designed to the ISO/IEC 17024:2026
framework** — audit-ready by design — so that the credential is structurally
legitimate now and the body can pursue formal accreditation once operational
history and candidate data exist. Where a requirement depends on data or
governance that only accumulates after launch, this document says so plainly
rather than overstating readiness.

**A note on subject matter and sources.** This credential concerns **auditing an
information security management system**. Its methodology is drawn from ISO
19011:2026, *Guidelines for auditing management systems*; its audit criteria are
ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and its Amendment 1:2024. Supporting sources include ISO/IEC
27007 for ISMS-specific audit guidance, ISO/IEC TS 17012:2024 for remote auditing
methods, and ISO/IEC 17021-1 and ISO/IEC 27006 where the boundary with third-party
certification must be drawn.

Certidemy teaches the *structure and reasoning* of these standards in its own
words. It does not reproduce their normative text, does not use their marks, and
does not present itself as, or as endorsed by, ISO, IEC or any national standards
body. **A candidate must obtain the standards themselves to work as an auditor;
this credential certifies competence in applying them, not a substitute for
holding them.**

## 2. Purpose and scope

The ISMS-IA credential certifies that the holder can **plan, conduct, report and
follow up an internal audit of an information security management system built to
ISO/IEC 27001:2022** — using ISO 19011:2026 as the audit methodology and ISO/IEC
27001 as the audit criteria — including where that management system has AI
systems inside its scope, and where AI tooling is used in the conduct of the audit
itself.

The credential addresses the obligation ISO/IEC 27001 clause 9.2 places on every
organization operating an ISMS: to conduct internal audits at planned intervals,
by auditors selected so as to ensure the objectivity and impartiality of the audit
process. An organization cannot discharge that obligation using its certification
body's auditors.

**Its position among audit credentials is drawn by the guidance standard itself.**
ISO 19011:2026 states in its Introduction that it *concentrates on internal audits
(first party) and audits conducted by organizations on their external providers
and other external interested parties (second party)*, and that *ISO/IEC 17021-1
provides requirements for auditing management systems for third-party
certification*. This credential sits on the first side of that line.

### In scope

Audit programme design and risk-based prioritisation. Scope, criteria and
objective setting for an individual audit. Method selection, including on-site,
remote and hybrid auditing methods. Evidence gathering, the degree of verification
information carries, and sampling adequacy. Testing an Annex A control's operating
effectiveness against its Statement of Applicability claim. Interviewing as a
judgment about evidential value. Determining whether evidence constitutes a
nonconformity and whether findings are systemic. Classification against a declared
scheme, reporting, verification and closure. Feeding clause 9.3 management review.
Auditor objectivity and competence in a first-party setting. Auditing an ISMS
whose scope contains AI systems; using AI tooling within the audit and disclosing
it; and distinguishing ISMS conformity from AI management system conformity where
both are in scope.

### Out of scope — stated plainly

- **Competence to lead a third-party certification audit.** This credential is not
  a Lead Auditor credential and does not qualify the holder to conduct
  certification audits on behalf of a certification body. Those requirements are
  set by ISO/IEC 17021-1 and ISO/IEC 27006 and are addressed by neither this
  scheme nor its examination.
- **Performing the audit as a behaviour** — opening and closing meetings, managing
  a defensive auditee, leading an audit team. These are conduct, not cognition,
  and no selected-response instrument reaches them.
- **Generating audit artefacts from scratch** — writing the audit plan or drafting
  the nonconformity report as original documents.
- **Implementing an ISMS.** That is a different job with a different body of
  knowledge.
- **Technical security testing at engineering depth** — penetration testing, code
  review, configuration hardening. The auditor establishes that such testing
  happened and was competently performed, and works within Annex A control 8.34,
  which requires audit tests involving operational systems to be planned and
  agreed with appropriate management.
- **Legal or regulatory compliance advice.** The credential certifies audit
  competence, not a statement of any organization's compliance status.

## 3. Eligibility

**No prerequisite is enforced.** Candidates are assumed to hold working knowledge
of ISO/IEC 27001 equivalent to a Foundation-level credential — the examination
does not test what the clauses say, it tests judgment about whether evidence
satisfies them, and a candidate without that grounding will find the questions
unreadable rather than merely difficult.

Certidemy's ISMS-F credential covers that ground; so does equivalent professional
experience or any comparable Foundation credential. **Certidemy does not require
its own training as a condition of examination.** Candidates may prepare by any
means.

No employment history, sponsorship or professional membership is required.

## 5. Competency model and job-task basis

The scheme rests on a documented **job-task analysis** (`ISMS-IA_JTA_v2.0`),
which defines every domain, task, cognitive level and concept, and from which the
examination blueprint is computed rather than asserted.

**Domains, tasks, concepts and examination items:**

| Domain | Weight | Tasks | Concepts | Items |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | 12.5% | 5 | 28 | 6 |
| D2 | 20.0% | 7 | 32 | 10 |
| D3 | 25.0% | 9 | 37 | 12 |
| D4 | 25.0% | 10 | 45 | 13 |
| D5 | 17.5% | 7 | 27 | 9 |
| **Total** | **100%** | **38** | **169** | **50** |

**Cognitive profile, computed from the task inventory:**

| Level | Share |
|---|---|
| 1 Remember | — |
| 2 Understand | 5.00% |
| 3 Apply | 29.40% |
| 4 Analyze | **65.60%** |
| 5 Evaluate | — |

**This profile is the scheme's central claim, and it is computed, not chosen.**
Bloom level is a declared property of each task, assigned from the task statement
and its knowledge, skills and abilities; the profile is derived from the task
inventory by a database view and is not written into this document by hand.

**Why Analyze dominates.** Auditing *is* analysis. The competence is to decompose
a situation into evidence, criteria and the relationships between them, and to
determine what conclusion the evidence supports. A profile weighted toward
Understand would describe a course *about* auditing rather than a credential in
it.

**No task in this scheme is assessed above Analyze.** Where a competence requires
generating an artefact or performing an act, it is placed out of scope at §2
rather than assessed by an instrument that cannot reach it.

## 6. Examination structure

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Items | 50 |
| Duration | 90 minutes |
| Seconds per item | 108 |
| Format | **Single best answer among four defensible options** |
| Scoring | Dichotomous — one key, no partial credit |
| Delivery | Closed book, online, secure |
| Languages | English, es-419, pt-BR |
| Pass mark | 75% (provisional — see §7) |
| Blueprint | Computed from domain weights; see §5 |

### The Level II item contract

A Level I item presents one defensibly correct answer among three that are wrong
on the merits. **A Level II item presents four options that are all defensible on
the facts given, one of which is best.** The candidate is not asked to recall what
a clause says; they are asked to determine what a situation means.

Two constraints govern every item:

1. **The best answer must be better than the second-best for a reason a competent
   auditor could state in one sentence.** If the reason takes a paragraph, the
   item is a coin flip.
2. **The second-best answer must be genuinely defensible, not merely wrong.** An
   item whose second choice is incorrect is a Level I item that reached the wrong
   bank.

Scoring remains dichotomous. The difficulty lives in item construction, not in a
partial-credit model, so the score a candidate receives means exactly what a Level
I score means.

### Sampling density

50 items across 38 tasks gives **1.32 items per task**. This is a deliberate design
choice rather than an accident of exam length: the body of knowledge is
narrower than a Foundation syllabus and the tasks are correspondingly fewer, which
allows better per-task coverage than a broader credential at the same exam length
can achieve. **Certidemy states this ratio because an auditor will calculate it.**

## 7. Pass mark and standard-setting

**The pass mark is 75% and is provisional.** It is **criterion-referenced** and
set by **expert judgment**: it expresses the proportion of this examination's
content a minimally competent internal auditor is expected to answer correctly.

**Why it is not 80%, the Certidemy Level I convention.** A cut score is a
statement about expected performance on *these items*. Level II items are
constructed so that a minimally competent candidate finds the second-best answer
genuinely attractive — that is the instrument working as designed. Carrying a
Level I number across a change in item construction would be a figure travelling
without its reasoning. The bar is not lower; the items are different.

**A formal standard-setting study is pending and named as an open item (§11).**
Once sufficient candidate data exists, a recognised method — modified Angoff or
bookmark — will be applied by a panel of subject-matter experts, and the cut score
will be re-derived from evidence rather than judgment. Any change will be
versioned and published. **The passing standard should be set from evidence rather
than opinion, and this document does not claim it yet has been.**

## 8. Assessment integrity and separation of functions

### 8.1 The secure firewall

The examination item bank and the practice item bank are **structurally separated**.
Secure items carry **zero** concept links, which is the mechanism by which practice
content is retrieved. A single link would expose examination content through the
practice path. **The firewall is a query, not a policy**: the count of secure items
carrying a concept link must be zero, and it is verified before publication and on
every subsequent verification run.

### 8.2 Item-bank floors

**Not fewer than 8 secure and 10 practice items per task, per language**, across
English, es-419 and pt-BR — a minimum of 912 secure and 1,140 practice items.
Over-fill is retained; items are never deleted to meet a number.

### 8.3 Answer-cue neutrality

Every item passes an automated cue guard measuring option-length homogeneity,
key-position uniformity, and the presence of rhetorical or absolute-word tells.

**The guard is adapted for Level II.** In a single-correct-answer item, an
unusually long key is a cue to remove. In a best-answer item, the best option is
frequently best *because* it is better qualified — and a guard tuned for Level I
would systematically reject correct items and retain flat ones. The Level II guard
therefore tests comparable **qualification density** across options rather than
comparable length.

### 8.4 Content attribution control

The item generator operates under an explicit attribution rule: it may not assert
that a standard requires something the standard does not state. **This credential
sits on the boundary between three documents** — ISO 19011 (guidance, no
requirements), ISO/IEC 27001 (requirements, but silent on much of audit practice)
and ISO/IEC 17021-1 (third-party certification). Widely-repeated audit maxims
frequently attach to none of them.

The job-task analysis records these boundaries explicitly and the examination
tests them as a competence in their own right, because an auditor who raises a
nonconformity against a requirement that does not exist has done real damage.

### 8.5 Separation of functions — stated honestly

Certidemy currently develops the training content, authors the item banks and
makes the certification decision. **A governance-level structure separating the
certification decision from the commercial and training functions is an open item
(§11)** and is required for accreditation. The structural controls above operate
now; the governance separation does not yet.

### 8.6 Use of AI in the certification process

Certidemy uses AI systems in the generation of instructional content, examination
items and translations, under human review and the controls described above.
**ISO/IEC 17024:2026 clause 6.5 addresses the use of artificial intelligence in
the certification process. Certidemy does not claim conformity with clause 6.5**,
which is assessed by an accreditation body. A documented AI-in-certification
policy and candidate-facing disclosure are named as open items (§11).

## 9. Recertification

The credential is valid for **1 year from issuance**.

The validity period reflects the **content re-review cadence** rather than an
estimate of how quickly the holder's competence decays. The body of knowledge sits
on standards under active revision: ISO 19011 moved to a fourth edition in May
2026, ISO/IEC 27007 has a revision in progress, and ISO/IEC 27090 is at Final
Draft. A credential asserting a five-year currency against that background asserts
something it cannot support.

Recertification is by re-examination against the then-current scheme version.
Issued credentials remain valid for their full term regardless of subsequent scheme
changes.

## 10. Traceability and coverage

The scheme maintains an unbroken chain: **domain → task → concept → lesson →
item**. Three properties are provable by query rather than asserted:

1. **Coverage.** Every concept in the body of knowledge is taught by at least one
   lesson, in every language offered. The count of concepts tested but not taught
   must be **zero**.
2. **Firewall isolation.** The count of secure items carrying a concept link must
   be **zero** (§8.1).
3. **Blueprint conformance.** Every assembled examination form satisfies the
   domain weighting in §5, and the published cognitive profile is computed from
   the same task inventory the blueprint is computed from — so the claim and the
   measurement cannot drift apart.

Each item traces to exactly one task; each task traces to one domain; each concept
traces to at least one task and at least one lesson. **The job-task analysis is
published**, so a candidate or an auditor can compare what is claimed against what
is assessed.

## 11. Scheme maintenance and governance

**Review cadence.** Reviewed when a source standard is materially revised, when
the job-task analysis is re-validated, or at minimum annually. Named
re-verification triggers at the time of writing: publication of **ISO/IEC 27090**
(at Final Draft, ballot closed August 2026), publication of the **ISO/IEC 27007**
revision, and any amendment to ISO/IEC 27001:2022 or ISO 19011:2026.

**Change control.** Changes to domains, weightings, task codes, the item bank or
the pass mark are versioned and recorded. Task codes are immutable once published.

**Standards-identity line (scope discipline).** ISO 19011, ISO/IEC 27001, ISO/IEC
27007, ISO/IEC 17021-1 and ISO/IEC 42001 are public International Standards used
as the foundational body of knowledge and named in lesson content as pedagogical
reference. They are **not** claimed as Certidemy's own. This credential is **not**
presented as, equivalent to, or endorsed by ISO, IEC or any national standards
body, and holding it is not a statement of any organization's compliance.

**Job-task analysis review status.** The job-task analysis underwent four rounds
of independent second-reviewer critique, one of which materially changed the task
inventory and its cognitive levels. **That is editorial rigour. It is not the
independent SME-panel validation ISO/IEC 17024:2026 requires**, which needs a
convened panel of practising internal auditors and is named below.

**Open items on the path to accreditation** — named transparently:

- Independent SME-panel validation of the job-task analysis and weightings (§5).
- Formal standard-setting study and psychometric validation of the cut score, from
  candidate data collected in live operation (§7).
- Item difficulty and discrimination statistics from live candidate data (§8).
- Governance-level impartiality structure separating the certification decision
  from the commercial and training functions (§8.5).
- Documented AI-in-certification policy and candidate-facing disclosure, per
  ISO/IEC 17024:2026 clause 6.5 (§8.6).
- Operational records required by the standard: appeals, complaints handling,
  examination-integrity and identity procedures, and internal audit of the
  certification body itself.
- Full lesson localization into es-419 and pt-BR.

**Open content note.** Examination and practice item banks are complete in all
three languages before publication. Instructional lessons are authored in English
first and localized progressively; a candidate may sit the examination and
practice in any offered language while lesson localization completes. This is
stated rather than implied as complete.

**Publication status.** This credential is **pre-launch** at the time of writing
(`status = draft`). The scheme spine is built and verified: 5 domains, 38 tasks,
169 concepts and the computed examination blueprint are live, and the structural
invariants above are confirmed by query — domain weights, task-to-domain
integrity, concept linkage, the secure firewall, the MCQ ceiling, and agreement
between the published blueprint and the cognitive profile computed from the
job-task analysis. The teaching layer and both item banks are not yet authored,
and the verification run reports them as outstanding rather than passing.
Publication follows their completion and the clearing of the launch checklist.

The credential is positioned as **designed to the ISO/IEC 17024:2026 framework —
audit-ready by design**, not as accredited, until accreditation is formally
achieved.
