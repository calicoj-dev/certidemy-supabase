## Why this document exists

`SCHEME-AIHR-I.md` §11.2 requires every Domain 2 lesson to carry an explicit
"verified as of" date and treats the domain's content as perishable by design. This
record is what those dates point at. It is also the reason the scheme's task
statements name no statute: three of the instruments below moved in the eighteen
months before this was written, and two more are mid-passage now.

**This is not legal advice and Domain 2 does not teach it as such.** Every lesson
built from this record terminates at the escalation boundary (§11.2 Rule 4).

## 1. Brazil

### In force today

**LGPD — Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados (Lei 13.709/2018).** This is the operative
instrument for AI in hiring in Brazil right now, and Domain 2 should teach it as such.

What matters for employment decisions:
- Automated processing of personal data in recruitment is squarely covered.
- **The right to request review of decisions taken solely on the basis of automated
  processing** that affect a person's interests — including decisions built on
  profiling — is the provision that most directly reaches AI-assisted selection.
- **ANPD** (Autoridade Nacional de Proteção de Dados) is the enforcing authority.

### Pending, not law

**PL 2338/2023** — the comprehensive AI framework.

| Stage | Date |
|---|---|
| Introduced in the Senate | May 2023 |
| Senate plenary approval | 10 December 2024 |
| Remitted to the Chamber of Deputies | 17 March 2025 |
| Referred to a special committee | 2025 |
| Status at verification | **Still pending in the Chamber; expected to move toward a vote before the August 2026 recess** |

Shape: risk-based, modelled on the EU AI Act, classifying systems from minimal to
excessive risk, prohibiting excessive-risk systems, and requiring impact assessments
for high-risk applications. Recruitment is among the applications it reaches. ANPD is
proposed as coordinating regulator, with penalties up to BRL 50 million or 2% of gross
turnover.

**Teaching position:** direction of travel, not present duty. The text remains subject
to amendment in the Chamber, and if amended it returns to the Senate. Any lesson that
states PL 2338 obligations as current is wrong.

## 2. Colombia

### In force today

**Ley 1581 de 2012** (habeas data / personal data protection) is the general
instrument, enforced by the **Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio (SIC)**. It
predates generative AI and applies anyway: collecting, storing, cross-referencing,
analysing, inferring from, or profiling personal data triggers its duties regardless
of the technology involved.

**SIC Circular Externa 002 of 21 August 2024** is the AI-specific instrument and the
most useful single item for Domain 2. It requires that processing of personal data in
AI systems satisfy **idoneidad, necesidad, razonabilidad y proporcionalidad**, and
requires a **privacy impact study where high risk exists**. This is a real, in-force,
AI-specific obligation reaching hiring tools — not a proposal.

**MinTIC Lineamientos de Seguridad y Privacidad para Inteligencia Artificial**, issued
**22 April 2026**: good-practice guidance on protecting data used by AI systems,
role-based access control, traceability of actions taken within the tool, and
separation of responsibilities in critical processes.

**Constitutional route.** Beyond regulatory sanction, a candidate or employee who
shows discrimination has the **tutela** available alongside ordinary labour claims.
For a Colombian employer this is often the more immediate exposure, and it is
particularly live for candidates with disabilities, migrant workers, and others under
special constitutional protection.

**Vendor responsibility does not transfer.** Colombian commentary is consistent that
contracting a third-party AI tool does not displace the deploying organization's
obligations — the responsable/encargado allocation, international transfers, security
measures, audit, retention, and complaint-handling all remain the deployer's to get
right. This maps directly onto task 2.9.

### Pending, not law

- **PL 043 de 2025** — comprehensive AI bill, before Congress. Risk-based, with
  MinCiencias leading and SIC retaining the data-protection, consumer and competition
  role.
- **A data-protection reform bill** modernizing Ley 1581 toward GDPR alignment.

## 3. Mexico

### In force today

**Ley Federal de Protección de Datos Personales en Posesión de los Particulares
(LFPDPPP 2025).** Published in the DOF **20 March 2025**, in force **21 March 2025**,
**repealing the 2010 law of the same name**.

What changed that matters here:
- **The regulator is now the Secretaría Anticorrupción y Buen Gobierno.** **INAI was
  abolished** by the constitutional reform published 20 December 2024.
- The definition of *tratamiento* is expanded and **explicitly covers automated
  procedures** applied to personal data.
- *Aviso de privacidad* must be given **from the moment data is collected**.
- ARCO rights procedures must accommodate requests concerning automated decisions.

> ### ⚠ Source-quality warning
>
> A large amount of 2026 Spanish-language compliance material still names **INAI** as
> the authority enforcing Mexican data protection, and some attributes specific fine
> ranges to it. **INAI does not exist.** Any source making that claim is describing a
> regime that ended in March 2025 and must not be used for lesson content. Verify
> Mexican material against the DOF text or a major law firm's post-March-2025
> analysis. This is the clearest illustration in this record of why §11.2 exists.

## 4. What Domain 2 can and cannot claim

**Can be taught as current duty:**
- Brazil: LGPD, including the right to review of automated decisions; ANPD as authority.
- Colombia: Ley 1581; SIC Circular Externa 002/2024 including the high-risk privacy
  impact study; MinTIC guidance of April 2026; tutela as a parallel route.
- Mexico: LFPDPPP 2025; automated processing expressly within *tratamiento*; Secretaría
  Anticorrupción y Buen Gobierno as authority.

**Must be taught as pending or directional:**
- Brazil PL 2338/2023 · Colombia PL 043 de 2025 and the Ley 1581 reform.

**Must not appear anywhere:**
- INAI as a current Mexican authority.
- Any statutory citation in a *task statement* — statutes live in lesson bodies and
  concept descriptions only (§4 Rule 1).

---

## 5. The teaching point this record produces

The obligation taxonomy in task 2.2 — disclosure, audit, explanation, human review,
record, non-discrimination liability — is what makes this manageable, and the LATAM
picture proves the design.

Brazil, Colombia and Mexico have **no employment-AI statute between them.** They have
data-protection law that reaches AI hiring anyway, one AI-specific regulatory circular,
one set of ministerial guidelines, a constitutional discrimination route, and two
pending bills. A learner who memorized "the LATAM AI rules" would have memorized
nothing usable. A learner who can ask *which of the six duties attaches here, under
what instrument* can work in all three countries and will still be able to when the
pending bills land.

That is the argument for the taxonomy, and Domain 2 should make it explicitly using
these three jurisdictions.
