Nothing is written to `task_sources` by this script. Generated by

## How to read it

| mark | what it means | how much to trust it |
|---|---|---|
| **cited** | the task's own knowledge statement names this clause | the blueprint's author anchored it; strongest evidence here |
| **names the title** | the task statement contains a phrase from the clause's own title | strong -- an author writing a task names the clauses |
| body overlap | the task and the clause share distinctive vocabulary | weak. Arithmetic, not judgement |

Task 2.3 reads *"Explain leadership requirements, the AI policy, AI objectives and
planning of changes"* -- and those are the NAMES of clauses 5.1, 5.2, 6.2 and 6.4. That is
why the title is the primary signal: it is the author pointing at the clause. Coverage is
measured against the title, so naming the whole of a two-word title outranks matching
three words of a five-word one.

Body overlap is weighted by inverse document frequency and normalised by the **shorter**
side, so a long task cannot score by sharing common words with everything.

**A container is not a candidate.** Clause 3 is held both whole and as 3.1 to 3.26, and as
a whole block it shares vocabulary with every task in the certification. An item anchored
in "clause 3" is anchored in the definitions section rather than in the definition it
relies on, so a passage whose subclauses are also held is excluded and its children stand.

**What this cannot do:** it cannot tell relevance from vocabulary. Two clauses about
planning share planning words; only one of them may be what a task examines. That is
the judgement being asked of you.

**Title matches are NOT capped; body-overlap candidates are, at 6.** Making the two
compete for the same slots lost clause 5.2 on task 2.3 twice while I was building this.
The full ranking is in `AIMS-F-TASK-SOURCES.json`, because a cap is a claim that nothing
below it matters.
