## About this document

This is the **certification scheme** for the AI for Human Resources & Talent I
credential: the published contract describing what the credential certifies, how a
candidate earns it, and how Certidemy keeps that decision fair, consistent, and
defensible. It is written to align with the structure of the ISO/IEC 17024
framework for bodies operating certification of persons.

**A note on accreditation status.** Certidemy is **not currently an accredited
certification body**. This scheme is designed to the ISO/IEC 17024 framework —
"audit-ready by design" — so that the credential is structurally legitimate now and
the body can pursue formal accreditation once operational history and candidate data
exist. Where a requirement depends on data or governance that only accumulates after
launch, this document says so plainly rather than overstating readiness.

**A note on subject matter.** This credential concerns the competent use of AI in
**employment decisions** — hiring, selection, and worker management. Its body of
knowledge draws on public, citable instruments (the EU AI Act and its Digital
Omnibus amendments, US federal anti-discrimination law and published agency guidance,
state and municipal employment-AI statutes, the NIST AI Risk Management Framework,
ISO/IEC 42001, and Latin American data-protection regimes). Certidemy teaches the
*structure and reasoning* of these instruments in its own words; it does not
reproduce their normative text and does not present itself as, or as endorsed by,
any standards body, regulator, or professional association.

**A note on what this credential is not.** AIHR-I is not a legal qualification and
does not license the holder to give legal advice. Its Domain 2 competence is
**role-level legal literacy for employment decisions** — the ability to recognize
which obligations are in play, act correctly within them, and escalate. Every
instructional unit in that domain terminates at the escalation boundary. This
distinction is binding on all marketing and catalog copy for this credential (§11.3).

## 2. Purpose and scope

The AIHR-I credential certifies that the holder can use AI in the employment
lifecycle **without creating legal exposure, without outsourcing judgment they
remain accountable for, and without being misled by either a vendor's capability
claims or a candidate's.**

**In scope:** identifying where AI touches employment decisions and classifying
whether a given tool falls inside the regulatory perimeter; recognizing which
categories of legal obligation attach in a given jurisdiction and acting correctly
within them; recognizing how AI-assisted selection produces discriminatory outcomes
absent any intent; defining role requirements as observable capability rather than
tool familiarity; judging what a credential or a claimed AI skill actually evidences;
and using AI responsibly in the practitioner's own recruiting workflow.

**Out of scope:** organizational AI governance programs, risk registers, and
assurance structures (**AIGRM-I**); general AI literacy for non-specialists
(**AIE-I**); model development, data science, and AI engineering; and legal advice
of any kind.

**Intended holders:** recruiters, talent acquisition specialists, HR business
partners, hiring managers, and HR operations staff — people who *make or materially
influence employment decisions*, not people who build or govern the systems.

**Relationship to the rest of the catalog.** AIE-I is the general AI-literacy floor
and is a recommended, not required, precursor (§3). AIGRM-I governs AI systems at the
organizational level; AIHR-I is the deployer-side, role-level competence of one
function that operates a consequential-decision system. A person holding both is the
natural owner of an HR AI program. Neither credential claims the other's scope, and
neither is represented as conferring it.

## 3. Eligibility

There are **no formal prerequisites**. The credential is open to any candidate.

**AIE-I is recommended but not required.** AIHR-I assumes general AI literacy and
applies it to one profession's work; a candidate without that floor will find the
examination harder but is not barred from it. A formal prerequisite was considered
and rejected: it would gate a paid credential behind a free one, introduce an
enrollment failure mode, and add no assessment value, since the examination measures
the applied competence directly.

No professional experience, employment status, or membership is required. Certidemy
does not restrict eligibility by geography, employer, or prior training provider, and
does not require that a candidate have studied with Certidemy.

## 4. Body of knowledge

The body of knowledge is triangulated from public instruments, the credentialing
field's own published practice, and the day-to-day activity of the target role. It
is set out in full in `AIHR-I_JTA_v1_2.md` §2.

**Primary public instruments.** EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), particularly
the Annex III classification of employment and worker-management AI as high-risk, the
Article 50 transparency obligations, and the Article 4 AI-literacy duty, as amended
by the Digital Omnibus on AI; US federal anti-discrimination law (Title VII, the ADA)
and the Uniform Guidelines on Employee Selection Procedures; published agency guidance
on AI in employment; municipal and state employment-AI statutes covering bias audit,
notice, explanation, and human-review duties; the NIST AI Risk Management Framework
and ISO/IEC 42001 as voluntary diligence frameworks; and Latin American
data-protection regimes as they apply to automated employment decisions.

**Field knowledge is not ownable.** Covering the same subject matter as other
frameworks or programs is legitimate and expected. What Certidemy does not do is
reproduce another body's normative text, mirror its structure, adopt its proprietary
constructs, or make any claim of identity or equivalence with its program. See §11.3.

**A property specific to this scheme: the body of knowledge is perishable.** Between
February and July 2026 the EU deferred its employment high-risk obligations by
sixteen months, one US state repealed its AI act before it took effect and replaced
it with a structurally different statute, and another brought a new employment-AI
regime into force with implementing rules unfinalized. The scheme's response is
structural and is described in §11.2 — **task statements state durable capability and
never statutory content**, so that the assessed competence survives legislative churn
and only the instructional layer needs maintenance.

## 5. Competency model and job-task basis

The scheme rests on a documented job-task analysis: `AIHR-I_JTA_v1_2.md`, locked
25 July 2026 following external review.

**Structure:** 4 domains, 28 tasks. Every task carries a knowledge / skills /
abilities triple, a criticality rating, a frequency rating, and a declared cognitive
level. Every task is exam-scope.

| # | Domain | Weight | Items | Tasks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | AI in the Talent Lifecycle | 20% | 8 | 5 |
| 2 | Legal Exposure, Bias & Candidate Rights | 30% | 12 | 9 |
| 3 | Scoping Roles & Evaluating Capability Claims | 30% | 12 | 9 |
| 4 | Responsible AI Use in the Recruiter's Workflow | 20% | 8 | 5 |
| | **Total** | **100%** | **40** | **28** |

**Cognitive profile.** The scheme declares **no Remember-level tasks**. Task-level
distribution is 14% Understand, 64% Apply, 21% Analyze; the item-level target is
approximately 18 / 57 / 25. This is a deliberate departure from the literacy-tier
profile used by AIE-I and reflects what the role requires: a practitioner who can
recite a rule but cannot recognize its violation in their own screening criteria has
not been made competent.

**Item cognitive level is derived, not asserted.** Every item inherits its Bloom
level from the task it is written against, enforced at the database level by
`trg_item_bloom_matches_task`. An item cannot claim a cognitive level its parent task
does not declare.

**Task statements are assessment-valid by construction**, under two separate
constraints. First, every statement uses a verb an examination of this format can
actually measure; statements denoting production (write, draft, create, maintain) were
systematically removed at JTA v1.1, because an item written against a production verb
silently measures something narrower than the task claims. Second, every verb sits
**at or below its task's declared cognitive level** — no statement opens with a Bloom-5
verb such as *evaluate* or *judge* while the examination measures at Analyze or below,
because the statement is the published claim and a verb above the assessment overstates
it. Three statements failed the second constraint at scaffold and were corrected before
any content was authored; the check is an automated invariant, not a review habit. Where the underlying competence is
productive, the KSA skills line still records it and the instruction still teaches it;
only the *assessable claim* is narrowed. This boundary is stated rather than concealed
(see JTA §4 Rule 5 and the note under task 3.3).

**Pending:** independent subject-matter-expert panel validation of the job-task
analysis and its weightings. The JTA has had structured external review but not
SME-panel validation; this is named as an open item on the path to accreditation
(§11.5).

## 6. Examination structure

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Format | Multiple choice, single correct answer, scenario-stemmed |
| Items per form | 40 |
| Duration | 50 minutes |
| Delivery | Online, secure examination |
| Book policy | Closed book |
| Attempts | One included with the examination purchase; retake available |
| Blueprint | Fixed by domain weight: 8 / 12 / 12 / 8 |
| Languages | English, es-419, pt-BR — identical blueprint in each |

**Blueprint fidelity.** Domain weights land on whole item counts with no rounding.
Every form drawn satisfies the blueprint exactly; no form over- or under-represents a
domain relative to the published weighting.

**Form variation.** The secure item bank is built to a floor of 8 items per task per
language — 224 secure items per language, 672 in total — which substantially exceeds
any single form's requirement in every domain. Forms therefore vary candidate to
candidate, and no single form approaches exposure of the bank.

**Timing rationale.** 75 seconds per item. The scheme's item stems carry scenarios
and take real time to read; reading speed should not be a discriminating variable.
This allowance is more generous per item than the catalog's Scrum credentials
(67.5 s/item) and less than its literacy tier (108 s/item), which is the correct
position for an applied role credential. Duration is the one examination parameter
Certidemy expects to revise against live data: median completion time will be
reviewed after the first cohort.

## 7. Pass mark and standard-setting

**Pass mark: 80% (32 of 40 items).**

**This cut score is provisional and is stated as such.** It is the catalog's
house convention, applied for consistency across credentials. It has **not** been
derived from a formal standard-setting study, because standard-setting requires
candidate performance data that does not yet exist for this scheme. Certidemy states
this plainly rather than implying psychometric derivation.

Two facts a reader should hold together: the cut score is nominally identical to
AIE-I's, and this examination is materially harder per item, because its cognitive
profile is weighted to Apply and Analyze rather than Remember and Understand. That
difference is intentional and reflects the competence claimed. It is also the first
place to investigate if early pass rates prove unreasonable.

**Committed:** a formal standard-setting study (modified Angoff or equivalent
criterion-referenced method, with a panel of qualified subject-matter experts) once
sufficient live candidate data exists, and adjustment of the cut score if that study
indicates it. Item difficulty and discrimination statistics will be computed from the
same data and used to retire or repair items that do not perform.

## 8. Assessment integrity and separation of functions

**Secure and practice item pools are structurally separated.** Practice items are
linked into the concept graph and are reachable by the learning engine; secure
examination items carry **zero** concept links and are unreachable by any practice
path. This is not a policy — it is enforced by construction in the generator and
verified as an invariant that must return zero before publication.

**Instruction does not reveal the examination.** No secure item appears in any
lesson, practice session, or study material in any language. A candidate who has
studied every lesson has not seen a single examination item.

**Coverage is proven, not assumed.** Every task in the blueprint is taught before it
is tested, verified by a coverage query whose untaught-testing-violation count must be
zero (§10).

**Separation of functions.** Certidemy authors instruction and operates the
examination. The impartiality structure required by ISO/IEC 17024 — formal separation
of the certification decision from commercial and training functions — is named as an
open item (§11.5). It is not claimed as satisfied.

### 8.1 Answer-cue neutrality

A multiple-choice item must be answerable only by knowing the content, not by
exploiting surface cues a test-wise candidate can read without competence. This
scheme's banks are generated under the platform's shared cue-neutrality control,
which for every item injects length-parity and no-positional-habit constraints into
authoring, audits and repairs or drops items whose key dominates on length or shows
the absolute-word tell, and deterministically shuffles option order so the correct
answer lands in a uniformly random slot. Cues are neutralized on the English skeleton,
so all three languages inherit a clean item.

The resulting distribution is measured and reproducible against the live secure pool
at any time. Target: approximately 25% of correct answers in each of the four option
positions, and no material key-longest bias. Figures for this scheme will be recorded
here once its banks are generated.

### 8.2 The Domain 3 impartiality control *(specific to this scheme)*

Domain 3 teaches candidates how to evaluate credentials. Certidemy issues
credentials. This is a genuine conflict of interest and is managed structurally
rather than by disclaimer.

**The rules.** Certidemy is never named anywhere in Domain 3 — not in instruction,
not in items, not in examples. ISO/IEC 17024 is named factually as one accreditation
standard among several and is never presented as the mark of a good credential. The
domain teaches evaluation *properties*, not a preferred category: university
certificates, vendor certifications, accredited certifications, completion badges,
and assessed micro-credentials are each taught for what they evidence and what they
do not, with none presented as superior in the abstract. Every worked example and item
stem uses hypothetical or generically described credentials, never a real program —
Certidemy's or anyone else's. Domain 3 is capped at 30% of the examination and the
credential-literacy tasks within it (3.5–3.8) at 15%; these are limits, not targets.

**The control that enforces them.** Instruction and items on tasks 3.5–3.8 are scanned
across all three languages before publication and again before any item-bank
regeneration, for: the strings `Certidemy` and `CertiGlobal` and every catalog
certification code (expected count **zero**); any real-world credential or training
provider name (expected count **zero**, confirmed by review of the flagged set rather
than by string match alone); and comparative-superiority phrasing about credential
*types*, which is flagged for human review rather than auto-failed, since legitimate
comparative teaching exists. A non-zero count on either of the first two blocks
publication.

**This control runs in the same verification pass as the secure-firewall and coverage
proofs.** It is not a separate review ritual. A rule that runs is a rule; a rule in a
document is a hope.

## 9. Recertification

**Credential validity: 1 year.**

The period is set by house policy across Certidemy credentials, and the reason
concerns the **certification's content** rather than the candidate's memory. A
validity period is a commitment to re-review the body of knowledge on that cadence
and to reissue only against material that still holds. Where a credential's subject
matter is anchored to a stable framework, that review may confirm the existing
version repeatedly and the scheme will hold a version for a long time. Where it is
not, the review will change things.

For AIHR-I the commitment is not notional. In the six months before this scheme was
written, the EU deferred its employment high-risk obligations by sixteen months, one
US state repealed its AI act before it took effect and replaced it with a
structurally different statute, and another brought a new regime into force with
implementing rules unfinalized. A credential asserting current competence across
that landscape cannot honestly run for two years, and the perishability controls in
§11.2 exist because of the same facts.

**Recertification is by re-examination** against the then-current form. Certidemy
does not offer continuing-education-credit recertification for this credential,
because the thing most likely to have gone stale is precisely the material a
candidate would need to be re-tested on rather than merely exposed to.

**Issued credentials are permanent records.** Expiry changes what a credential
asserts about currency; it does not revoke the fact that the holder passed a
specified examination on a specified date. Expired credentials remain verifiable as
expired. A credential is never deleted, and a learner cannot remove one by
unenrolling — unenrollment is archival, per the record-retention posture the ISO/IEC
17024 framework expects.

| Translations reviewed | no `is_provisional` translation rows | `i18n.approved` |
| Domain 3 impartiality control | zero prohibited strings on tasks 3.5–3.8 | §8.2 scan |

### 10.4 Terminology and translation policy

The scheme is delivered in English, es-419, and pt-BR. Its job-task analysis — every
task statement and every domain title and description — is published in all three
languages, because **a task statement is the exact wording an item is built against**
and a candidate sitting in Spanish or Portuguese is entitled to read the same claim
the item measures.

**Governing rules.** The platform-wide `TERMINOLOGY-POLICY.md` applies in full. The
rules most load-bearing for this scheme:

- **Bloom verbs map one-to-one and stay put.** The verb carries the cognitive level
  and `bloom_level` is checked against it; a more natural synonym is not an
  improvement.
- **Regulatory terms follow the official language versions of the instrument**, not
  literal translation. The EU AI Act exists in Spanish and Portuguese and governs
  statutory terms — *responsable del despliegue* / *responsável pela implantação* for
  deployer, and so on. A scheme grounded in an instrument should not diverge from it
  on the instrument's own vocabulary.
- **Named public frameworks get their English glossed once**, on first use in the
  defining task only. Field-standard concepts are not glossed.
- **Domain titles use sentence case** in es-419 and pt-BR. Title Case is an English
  orthographic convention.
- **AI is `IA`** in both target languages; English acronyms are carried in parentheses
  on first use only where the target language has no established form of its own.

**Provisional-until-reviewed.** AI-drafted translations enter the database with
`is_provisional = true` and are flipped only after external review. That column is a
governance artifact: the scheme can state truthfully that machine-drafted translations
are marked provisional until a reviewer signs off, and the flag proves it is enforced
rather than asserted. `verify-cert.mjs` invariant `i18n.approved` blocks publication
while any provisional row remains.

**Staleness cannot accumulate silently.** `trg_invalidate_task_translations` and
`trg_invalidate_domain_translations` flip a row's translations back to provisional
**in the same transaction** that changes its English statement or domain text. There
is no path around it short of disabling the trigger. Together with `i18n.approved`,
this makes the failure mode that damaged SM-AI-I structurally impossible: the English
cannot move without the translations declaring themselves stale, and the credential
cannot ship while they are.

**The translation source is the database, never a hand-written document.** The
authoritative rendering of this JTA for any downstream purpose — translation,
review, publication — is generated from the live schema. Hand-written narrative
(rationale, sourcing, review history) remains hand-written and is not a source of
task statements.

## 11. Scheme maintenance and governance

### 11.1 Production sequence commitments

Two ordering commitments are made here because both correspond to failures this
platform has already paid for.

**JTA translation happens at scaffold, not at the end.** The es-419 and pt-BR
renderings of all 28 task statements and all 4 domain titles and descriptions are
produced, externally reviewed, and approved **in the same work wave that creates the
task rows** — before lesson authoring begins, and long before item generation.

The reason is specific. Across the first six credentials, JTA translation was deferred
to the end of the arc. By the time it ran, English statements had been revised by
intervening corrections, and translations generated before those corrections were
silently stale — five statements in one credential, one in another. The root cause was
worse than late timing: the translator read a hand-written markdown document that had
drifted from the database, and a later audit found 76 task statements contradicting
the database and 16 tasks absent from their documents entirely. Retrofitting cost a
full session.

For this scheme that failure mode is closed at both ends: the English is **locked**
before scaffold, so there is no "the statements might still move" justification for
deferring; and the invalidation triggers make any later movement self-declaring. There
is no remaining reason to defer, and this scheme does not.

**Lesson localization remains progressive and is not a launch blocker**, provided the
open-content note in §11.6 states the position honestly. Once the item banks are
trilingual a candidate may examine and practise in any of the three languages while
lesson localization catches up. The JTA layer is not covered by that allowance.

### 11.2 Content currency

Domain 2 is the platform's first genuinely perishable content and is treated as such
from the outset rather than retrofitted.

- **Task statements carry no statutory content.** Jurisdiction, statute name,
  effective date, and threshold live only in instructional content and concept
  descriptions, which are updatable. A change of law does not invalidate a task code.
- **Every Domain 2 instructional unit carries an explicit "verified as of" date** in
  its frontmatter and a scheduled re-verification.
- **The currency claim is scoped to that date and never to "current law."**
- **Domain 2 teaches an obligation taxonomy** — disclosure, audit, explanation, human
  review, record, non-discrimination liability — rather than a statute list, so a
  holder can read a new regime by its shape. This is the property that makes the
  competence durable while the instruments beneath it move.

### 11.3 Identity and non-affiliation

Certidemy builds on public bodies of knowledge and states so. It does not present
itself as, or as endorsed by, any regulator, standards body, professional association,
or other certification program, and does not present another program's syllabus or
domain structure as its own. Marketing and application chrome for this credential
must not make or imply any claim of identity or equivalence with a third-party
program.

**The Domain 2 positioning phrase is binding on all copy:** *role-level legal literacy
for employment decisions*. Never "governance," never "compliance certification," and
never any phrasing implying the holder can clear a legal question. The credential's
30% Domain 2 weighting is defensible only while that phrasing holds; if copy drifts
toward governance language, either the copy or the weighting must change.

### 11.4 Change control

Task codes and statements are immutable once published. A substantive change to the
competency model requires a new JTA version, external review, a re-lock, and a scheme
document revision — not an edit. Domain weights, blueprint, and cut score are recorded
here and changed only through the same route. Duration and validity are examination
parameters and may be revised against operational data with the change recorded in
this document.

### 11.5 Open items on the path to accreditation

Named plainly rather than implied as satisfied:

- Independent SME-panel validation of the job-task analysis and its weightings (§5).
- Formal standard-setting study and psychometric validation of the cut score (§7).
- Item difficulty and discrimination statistics from live candidate data (§7, §8.1).
- Governance-level impartiality structure separating the certification decision from
  commercial and training functions (§8).
- Operational records required by the standard: appeals, complaints handling,
  examination-integrity and identity procedures, and internal audit.
- Latin American regulatory content authored into Domain 2 — **a launch requirement
  for this credential, not a fast-follow** (§11.6).

### 11.6 Open content note

At the time of writing this credential is **pre-production**: the job-task analysis is
locked, no instruction is authored, and no item bank exists. Nothing in this document
should be read as describing a live credential.

Two conditions must hold before publication, beyond the ordinary build gates:

1. **Latin American regulatory coverage.** Domain 2 must carry Brazilian and Colombian
   material at launch, at minimum. This platform serves Latin American markets first;
   an employment-AI credential teaching only European and United States regimes would
   be incomplete for the candidate it is sold to. This is a blocker.
2. **The Domain 3 impartiality control (§8.2) must pass** across all three languages.

When the credential publishes, this section states plainly what is complete and what
is progressing — including any gap between trilingual examination availability and
lesson localization — rather than implying uniform completeness.

## 12. Governance dashboard

Every guarantee in this scheme is designed to surface as a **live query** in the
super-admin governance view, so that a reviewer sees both the contract and its
current, queryable state in one place. The intended feeds are the nine rows of §10,
plus the standard-setting and item-statistics feeds, which read *pending live
candidate data* until the first cohort exists.

The §8.2 impartiality control is new with this scheme and should be added to the
standard verification pass rather than kept as a one-off, so that credential #8
inherits it without anyone having to remember.
