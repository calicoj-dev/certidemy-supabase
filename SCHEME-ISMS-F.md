## About this document

This is the **certification scheme** for the ISO/IEC 27001 Foundation - AI
credential: the published contract describing what the credential certifies, how a
candidate earns it, and how Certidemy keeps that decision fair, consistent, and
defensible. It is written to align with the structure of the ISO/IEC 17024:2026
framework for bodies operating certification of persons.

**A note on accreditation status.** Certidemy is **not currently an accredited
certification body**. This scheme is designed to the ISO/IEC 17024:2026 framework —
"audit-ready by design" — so that the credential is structurally legitimate now and
the body can pursue formal accreditation once operational history and candidate data
exist. Where a requirement depends on data or governance that only accumulates after
launch, this document says so plainly rather than overstating readiness.

**A note on the edition pin.** ISO/IEC 17024:2026 replaced the 2012 edition in March
2026. The edition is named explicitly throughout this document because the referent
is otherwise ambiguous while two editions are in circulation. The 2026 edition
introduced clause 6.5, on the use of artificial intelligence in the certification
process. **Certidemy has not assessed itself against clause 6.5 and makes no claim
of conformity to it.** That work — a documented AI-in-certification policy and a
candidate-facing disclosure — is named in the open-items list in §11.

**A note on subject matter and identity.** This credential concerns **ISO/IEC 27001
and information security management**, taught with AI woven through where the
practitioner's work genuinely changes. Certidemy is **not** ISO, is not authorized by
ISO, and does not reproduce ISO's normative text. The credential teaches the
*structure and reasoning* of the standard in Certidemy's own words. Candidates who
need the normative text must obtain the standard from ISO or an authorized national
body. See the standard-identity line in §11.

## 2. Purpose and scope

The ISMS-F credential certifies that the holder understands **ISO/IEC 27001 and the
operation of an information security management system**: the properties information
security protects, how asset, threat, vulnerability, risk and control compose, what a
management system is and how Clauses 4 to 10 fit together, how risk is assessed and
treated, what Annex A is for and how controls are selected, and how performance is
evaluated and certification obtained.

The distinguishing scope of this credential is that **AI is treated as first-class
throughout, and only where it genuinely changes the work.** An organization's estate
now contains models, agents, vector stores and prompt histories that its asset
register was not built to hold; its scope boundaries are crossed by browser-reached
services no procurement process saw; its access controls rest on assumptions a
non-human credential holder does not satisfy; and its monitoring cannot detect an
anomaly that is semantic rather than syntactic. This credential teaches the standard
in full **and** teaches where that estate strains it.

**Equally, it teaches where nothing changed.** The physical controls theme is
materially unaffected by AI adoption, and the scheme says so with its reasoning
rather than manufacturing an angle. A candidate who can state where a control is
*not* affected, and why, holds a competence this scheme deliberately assesses.

**Target audience.** Practitioners entering or working in information security,
governance, risk or compliance roles; IT, audit, legal and procurement professionals
whose work now touches an ISMS; and technical staff who need a working command of the
management-system frame around the systems they build or operate.

**Out of scope.** This credential does not certify the ability to implement an ISMS,
to lead or conduct a certification audit, or to give assurance on any organization's
conformity. It does not certify legal or regulatory compliance sign-off. It certifies
Foundation-level literacy and applied judgment. **It is not issued, endorsed, or
recognized by ISO**, and it does not confer any status under ISO/IEC 27001 itself.

## 3. Eligibility

The ISMS-F examination operates under **open eligibility**: there are no mandatory
prerequisites, formal training hours, or prior-credential requirements to attempt the
examination. This is a Foundation-tier credential, and the body has chosen open
access as a deliberate scheme policy consistent with Foundation-level practice across
the market.

Candidates are strongly advised to complete the associated body-of-knowledge
coursework (or to possess equivalent practical experience) before attempting the
secure examination, but completion of Certidemy coursework is **not** a condition of
eligibility and confers no advantage in the secure examination (§8).

## 4. Body of knowledge

The body of knowledge is organized into **5 domains** comprising **49 assessable
tasks**, supported by **191 defined concepts** and delivered through **5 instructional
modules / 49 lessons**, complete in all three languages (§11).

The five domains and their examination weightings:

| Domain | Title | Weight | Tasks | Lessons |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Information security fundamentals and the AI-era threat landscape | 15.0% | 7 | 7 |
| D2 | The ISMS: context, leadership, scope and policy with AI in the estate | 17.5% | 9 | 9 |
| D3 | Risk assessment and treatment | 22.5% | 11 | 11 |
| D4 | Annex A controls and the AI weave | 27.5% | 13 | 13 |
| D5 | Performance evaluation, improvement and certification | 17.5% | 9 | 9 |

(Domain titles, weights and task counts are reconciled against the live `domains` and
`tasks` rows for ISMS-F; weights sum to 100 and per-domain task counts sum to 49.)

All **49 tasks are within examination scope.** The heaviest weighting (D4) falls on
the Annex A control themes and where AI changes their application — the scheme's
signature area, and the one that most distinguishes it from a conventional Foundation
syllabus.

The body of knowledge is grounded in ISO/IEC 27001 and the wider ISO/IEC 27000 family,
in ISO 31000 risk-management vocabulary, and in published practitioner threat
catalogs for AI systems (OWASP GenAI Security Project, MITRE ATLAS, the NIST
adversarial-ML taxonomy). Those catalogs are **practitioner consensus, not standards**,
and the scheme teaches them as such: where any of them conflicts with ISO/IEC 27001,
the standard governs.

**A live fact the body of knowledge teaches.** ISO/IEC 27000:2026, published in July
2026, dropped "and vocabulary" from its title and reduced its terms clause from
roughly seventy-seven entries to about a dozen; definitions moved to the ISO Online
Browsing Platform and to the individual standards that use them. No lesson in this
scheme cites ISO/IEC 27000 as a definitional source, and task 1.4 teaches the change
itself.

## 5. Competency model and job-task basis

The domain-and-task structure is a **job-task model**: each of the 49 tasks describes
what a competent Foundation-level practitioner must know or do, and each task is
linked to the concepts it draws on and to the assessment items that test it. This
produces a complete, queryable **traceability matrix** from domain → task → concept →
assessment item → instructional lesson (§10).

**Cognitive profile.** The examination's cognitive distribution is not chosen; it is
**computed from the declared Bloom level of the 49 tasks** and enforced by a database
trigger that rejects any item whose level disagrees with its task:

| Level | Tasks | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Remember | 5 | 10.4% |
| Understand | 28 | 56.8% |
| Apply | 11 | 22.7% |
| Analyze | 5 | 10.2% |

This is a Foundation profile: predominantly Understand, with genuine Apply and Analyze
content concentrated in the AI-affected tasks. The four Analyze tasks — scope erosion
(2.5), blind-spot diagnosis (3.11), non-human identity (4.6), foundation-model
assurance limits (4.8) — and 5.8 on detection gaps are the scheme's hardest content
and are deliberately placed where the practitioner's judgment, not recall, is what
matters.

**Validation status — stated honestly.** The competency model was derived from ISO/IEC
27001 itself, from the wider 27000 family, and from analysis of what an information
security practitioner now does differently in an estate containing AI systems.
**Independent subject-matter-expert (SME) panel validation of the job-task analysis is
planned but not yet completed**, and is a prerequisite for accreditation. An external
reviewer pass was performed on the job-task analysis and on all 49 lessons during
authoring; that is editorial / second-reviewer rigor, not the formal SME job-task
validation the standard requires.

## 6. Examination structure

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Number of items | 40 |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Item format | Single-best-answer multiple choice and true/false |
| Delivery language | Candidate-selected: en, es-419, or pt-BR |
| Reference material | Closed book |
| Scoring | Dichotomous (correct / incorrect); no negative marking |

(The parameters above are confirmed against the live `certifications` row for ISMS-F.)

**Blueprint (item allocation by domain).** Each form draws items proportionally to the
domain weightings, yielding this target distribution at 40 items:

| Domain | Weight | Items per form (target) |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | 15.0% | 6 |
| D2 | 17.5% | 7 |
| D3 | 22.5% | 9 |
| D4 | 27.5% | 11 |
| D5 | 17.5% | 7 |

(Per-form counts follow directly from the live weights; the row total equals 40.)

The examination assembler draws each form from the secure item bank according to this
blueprint and **refuses to issue a form if any domain is short of its quota**,
guaranteeing every candidate receives a blueprint-valid form.

**Sampling — stated plainly.** A 40-item form over 49 assessable tasks samples at
**0.82 items per task**. **No single form can test every task, and no candidate should
expect one to.** The form is a valid sample of the domain structure, not a census of
the body of knowledge: domain weightings are honoured exactly, and within each domain
the assembler draws across that domain's tasks. Two consequences follow, and both are
deliberate.

First, **a candidate cannot predict which tasks will appear**, so preparation must
cover the published body of knowledge rather than a subset. Second, **every lesson is
written to stand alone as teaching** rather than as exam preparation for a task that
may not be sampled.

The 40-item, 60-minute form is the established Foundation-tier format in this subject
area, and the scheme adopts it deliberately rather than inflating the form to raise
the sampling ratio. The ratio is the lowest in the Certidemy catalog and is stated
here rather than left for a candidate to infer.

## 7. Pass mark and standard-setting

**Current pass mark:** 80% (32 of 40 items).

**Methodology — stated honestly.** The pass mark is **criterion-referenced**: it
reflects the level of mastery the body judges necessary to certify competence, not a
curve relative to other candidates. The current 80% threshold is a **provisional,
expert-judgment cut score**.

It has **not yet** been validated by a formal standard-setting study, because
defensible standard-setting (e.g., a modified-Angoff panel and statistical
confirmation against candidate performance) **requires real candidate data that does
not yet exist.** The body's plan is to collect candidate response data through live
operation, compute item-level statistics and form reliability, then convene a
standard-setting panel to confirm or adjust the cut score against that evidence. Until
then, the 80% pass mark is held as the documented, criterion-referenced standard and
applied uniformly to every candidate and form.

**A note on the interaction with sampling.** At 40 items, each item is worth 2.5% and
the pass boundary sits eight items from full marks. A short form raises the weight of
each item, which is a further reason the standard-setting study matters and is named
as an open item rather than assumed settled.

## 8. Assessment integrity and separation of functions

**Separation of training and certification.** Certidemy operates a **technical
separation** between the learning function and the certification function through a
strict item-pool firewall:

- The **practice pool** (used by the adaptive learning engine and study tools) and the
  **secure pool** (used only to assemble the live certification examination) are
  entirely distinct sets of items.
- Secure items are **never linked into the practice/learning graph**
  (`question_concepts`), so no secure examination content is reachable through study,
  review, or practice. The secure examination is **inert to the adaptive learning
  engine.** This is a DB-provable invariant: a firewall query returns zero secure items
  carrying a concept link (see §10).
- Completion of coursework confers **no advantage** on the secure examination and is
  not a condition of eligibility (§3).

**Governance-level separation** (an impartiality structure ensuring certification
decisions are free from commercial or training-side influence) is a formal
accreditation requirement on the body's roadmap; it is named here as
established-in-design, to-be-formalized-operationally.

**Item bank inventory.** The item bank holds, **per assessable task**, a floor of 8
secure items and 10 practice items **per language** across en / es-419 / pt-BR, for all
49 tasks:

| Pool | Floor per task per language | Design total (49×) | Current holdings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Secure (examination) | 8 | 1,176 | 1,176 (392 × 3 languages, verified) |
| Practice (learning) | 10 | 1,470 | 1,470 (490 × 3 languages, verified) |

Both banks are complete and balanced at exactly their floor across all 49 tasks in all
three languages. Verified by the completion-gate, balance and coverage queries (§10,
§12): every practice item is concept-linked (engine-reachable), every secure item is
firewall-isolated (zero links into the practice graph), and every question group holds
exactly its three language rows (882 groups).

**Form variation.** The secure bank provides substantial over-coverage of every
domain's blueprint quota — 392 items per language against a 40-item form — so forms
vary candidate-to-candidate and no single form approaches exposure of the bank.

**Item cognitive level.** Every secure item carries the Bloom level of the task it
assesses, enforced by a database trigger rather than by convention. Verified live:
1,176 secure items, all matching their task's JTA declaration.

### 8.1 Answer-cue neutrality (measured and corrected)

A multiple-choice item must be answerable **only by knowing the content** — not by
exploiting surface cues a test-wise candidate can read without competence. This
scheme's item bank was generated under an explicit answer-cue-neutrality control and
is reproducibly auditable.

**The control.** A single shared cue-neutrality module governs both generators. For
every item, before translation, it (a) injects length-parity / no-positional-habit /
no-rhetorical-tell rules into the authoring prompt, (b) audits the item and drops or
repairs any whose key dominates on length or shows the absolute-word tell (every
distractor carrying an absolute — always / never / only — while the key hedges), and
(c) deterministically shuffles option order (Fisher–Yates) so the correct answer lands
in a uniformly random slot. Cues are neutralized on the English skeleton, so all three
languages inherit a clean item. During ISMS-F generation the guard actively dropped
length-dominant and absolute-word-tell items and normalized option lengths across both
banks; the drops are visible in the generation logs.

**The result (ISMS-F secure pool).** Measured on the authored English skeleton (392
items) by the same cue-audit query used across the catalog:

| Correct-answer slot | Share |
|---|---|
| 1st | 24.4% |
| 2nd | 23.7% |
| 3rd | 23.9% |
| 4th | 28.0% |

Chi-square 1.9 against a 11.34 threshold: the correct answer is distributed
essentially uniformly across the four option positions, so a candidate cannot improve
their odds by favoring a position.

**Length cue.** The key is the strictly longest option in 40.4% of items, with a mean
margin of 5.4 characters — 4.8% of an option's length — and zero guard escapes across
389 measured items. A candidate reading option lengths gains nothing usable.

**Honest ceiling.** This control proves the item bank is **cue-neutral and structurally
sound**. It does **not** establish item difficulty or discrimination, which can only
come from live candidate response data and remain pending (§7).

**Credential verification.** Every issued credential carries a unique credential code
and a public verification record, allowing any third party to confirm a credential's
authenticity, holder, issue date, and validity status.

## 9. Recertification

ISMS-F credentials are valid for **1 year** from issuance.

**Rationale.** The credential certifies Foundation-level competence in an ISMS
operated in an estate containing AI systems — a subject where both halves move. The
ISO/IEC 27000 family is actively changing (the 2026 edition of 27000 restructured its
terms clause; ISO/IEC 27090, on security threats to AI systems, was at final draft
stage as this document was written), and the AI-side practice moves faster still. A
1-year cycle keeps a current credential a genuine signal of current competence.
Recertification re-tests against the then-current body of knowledge, which is itself
reviewed as the underlying standards evolve (§11).

**A note on market comparison.** Foundation credentials in this subject area are
commonly issued with three-year validity or none at all. Certidemy's 1-year cycle is
shorter deliberately, and reflects the content re-review cadence the body actually
operates rather than a commercial convention.

## 10. Traceability and coverage

The scheme maintains a complete, queryable traceability matrix:

**domain → task → concept → assessment item**, and
**concept → instructional lesson**

Three audit-relevant guarantees follow, each backed by a live database view or query
(these are the intended data feeds for the super-admin governance dashboard; see §12):

1. **Coverage** — every one of the 49 in-scope tasks is supported by assessment items
   in both pools, in all three languages (verified: every task carries ≥8 secure and
   ≥10 practice items per language), and is taught by at least one lesson. Proven live:
   **191 / 191 concepts taught, 191 / 191 concepts tested, 0 taught-not-yet-practiced.**
2. **No untaught testing** — every concept assessed is present in the published body of
   knowledge, and every examination item resolves back to the lesson(s) that teach its
   concept(s). Proven live: **untaught-testing violations = 0.** This is the ISO/IEC
   17024:2026 "no untaught testing" guarantee, queryable via the coverage view.
3. **Secure firewall** — the secure examination pool carries **zero** links into the
   practice/learning graph, provable by a firewall query returning 0 (verified this
   build; see §8).

**A note on what the coverage view measures.** The coverage view derives "tested" from
the practice/learning graph (`question_concepts`). Because secure items carry no such
link by design (the firewall), secure-pool sufficiency is proven not by the coverage
view but by the blueprint allocator plus a per-task count (every task at its
≥8-per-language floor, confirmed). Two pools, two proofs: practice reach by the
coverage view, secure sufficiency by the allocator and count.

The projection of lessons into this matrix (194 concept links and 49 task links across
49 lessons), and the generation of assessment items against the taught concepts, are
maintained by automated, re-runnable controls rather than by hand, so the matrix stays
complete as content evolves.

## 11. Scheme maintenance and governance

**Review cadence.** Reviewed when ISO/IEC 27001 or a governing member of the 27000
family is materially revised, when a relevant AI-security standard publishes
(ISO/IEC 27090 and 27091 are named watch items), when the job-task analysis is
re-validated, or at minimum annually.

**Volatility register.** The body of knowledge carries a register of items expected to
move, each with a named re-check trigger. Current entries include ISO/IEC 27090 and
27091 (final draft stage, not citable as requirements), ISO 31000 Edition 3 (committee
draft, and a source of Spanish risk-vocabulary change), and the published practitioner
threat catalogs, which revise on their own cadence.

**Change control.** Changes to domains, weightings, the item bank, or the pass mark are
versioned and recorded.

**Standard-identity line (scope discipline).** ISO/IEC 27001 and the wider ISO/IEC
27000 family are used as the **foundational body of knowledge** and named in lesson
content as pedagogical reference. Certidemy is **not** ISO, is not authorized or
endorsed by ISO, and does not reproduce ISO's normative text. Lessons teach the
structure and reasoning of the standard in Certidemy's own words; a candidate needing
the normative text must obtain the standard from ISO or an authorized national body.
This credential is a Certidemy credential. **It confers no status under ISO/IEC 27001
and is not an ISO certification of any kind.**

**Terminology discipline.** Because this credential is built on a standard with
adopted national translations, clause-and-control vocabulary in es-419 and pt-BR
follows the adopted standard rather than a general-purpose rendering — the test being
that a candidate can open the source and check. The rule and its verified glossary are
recorded in `TERMINOLOGY-ISMS-F.md` and are enforced in the translation tooling.

**Content localization.** Instructional lessons are **complete in all three languages**
— 49 lessons × en / es-419 / pt-BR, 147 rows, loaded and verified free of encoding
corruption. Both item banks are complete in all three languages. A candidate may study
and sit the examination fully in any of the three languages.

**Publication status.** This credential is **pre-launch** at the time of writing
(`status = coming_soon`): the teaching layer is complete and coverage-proven in all
three languages, and both the secure and practice item banks are complete, balanced and
verified. Publication to `available` follows once the open items below that gate launch
are cleared.

**Open items on the path to accreditation** (named transparently):

- **Independent review of the es-419 and pt-BR blueprint translations.** The 108 domain
  and task-statement translation rows are currently held as **provisional** and have not
  been through independent review. This gates publication: a candidate must not sit an
  examination whose published blueprint has not been read in their language. *(Verified
  live: `verify-cert --strict` fails on this and only this.)*
- Independent SME-panel validation of the job-task analysis and weightings (§5).
- Formal standard-setting study and psychometric validation of the cut score, using
  candidate data collected in live operation (§7).
- Item difficulty and discrimination statistics from live candidate data (§8.1).
- Governance-level impartiality structure separating the certification decision from
  commercial and training functions (§8).
- A documented **AI-in-certification policy and candidate-facing disclosure**, as
  ISO/IEC 17024:2026 clause 6.5 contemplates. Certidemy makes no claim of conformity to
  clause 6.5 until this exists.
- Operational records required by the standard: appeals, complaints handling,
  examination-integrity / identity procedures, and internal audit.

The credential is positioned as **"designed to the ISO/IEC 17024:2026 framework /
audit-ready by design,"** not as accredited, until accreditation is formally achieved.
